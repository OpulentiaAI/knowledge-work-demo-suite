Better eggs, the way nature intended.

Aurora Eggs is a family-owned New Zealand egg producer with farms on both the North and South Islands. We have been farming eggs in Aotearoa since 1954, and three generations of the McKenna family have shaped how we farm today. Our flock of around 610,000 layer hens produces fresh shell eggs every day for New Zealand families, cafés, restaurants and bakeries. We farm across colony, barn, and free-range systems - because we believe Kiwi consumers deserve choice - and we are investing in the next generation of barn and free-range housing to meet the country's 2027 cage-free expectations. We are proud to supply long-standing partners across both major New Zealand supermarket groups under supply agreements that go back decades.

Our Farms

Aurora farms eggs at two main sites in New Zealand: a North Island farm in the rolling Waikato countryside south of Auckland, and a South Island farm on the Canterbury Plains near Christchurch. Together our two farms are home to approximately 610,000 layer hens, making Aurora one of the largest dedicated egg producers in New Zealand.

Our North Island farm

Our Waikato farm has been in continuous operation since the late 1950s, when Tom McKenna moved the family operation from a single shed in West Auckland to a larger property where his sons could expand. Today the North Island site houses a mix of colony, barn, and free-range housing, with new free-range and barn capacity coming online as part of our cage-free investment programme.

Our South Island farm

We expanded south in 1989 to better serve our South Island retail and foodservice partners with locally-laid eggs. Our Canterbury farm sits on a 60-hectare site with its own grading and packing facility, supplying customers from Nelson down through Otago and Southland. The Canterbury Plains' temperate climate and pasture make it an ideal home for our growing free-range flock.

Three generations of farming

We are a third-generation family business. Every member of our extended family team - from the McKennas who own the company, to the farm managers and stockpersons who care for the hens day-in and day-out - shares one commitment: producing the best eggs in New Zealand, the way the McKennas have always done it.

Our Story

Aurora Eggs is a three-generation New Zealand family business. We started small - one shed, a few hundred birds, in West Auckland in 1954 - and grew, slowly and stubbornly, into one of New Zealand's largest dedicated egg producers. Through it all the company has stayed in the McKenna family, and the way we farm has stayed grounded in the values Tom set down in the 1950s.

Tom McKenna - first generation (1954)

Our founder, Tom McKenna, was a returned serviceman who came home from the Pacific in 1946 and bought a small block of land in Henderson, west Auckland. He started Aurora in 1954 with a single shed and a flock of around 400 hens. Tom believed that better-fed, better-housed hens laid better eggs - a simple idea that became the foundation of every Aurora egg laid in the seven decades since. Tom passed away in 1998 but his name is still on every carton.

The second generation (1970s-1990s)

Tom's son took the operation through its biggest period of growth. In the 1970s and 1980s the company moved from a single Auckland shed to the Waikato farm we still operate today, and in 1989 we opened our South Island site near Christchurch. By the early 1990s Aurora was supplying both major New Zealand supermarket groups and had become a household name in Kiwi kitchens.

Eleanor McKenna - Chair

Eleanor McKenna joined the family business in the early 1980s and has chaired Aurora's family board since 2005. Eleanor is the keeper of the company's values - she has overseen Aurora's transition out of battery cages, our investment in free-range and barn housing, and the modernisation of our supply relationships. She is the steady hand on the tiller.

Mark McKenna - third generation, Managing Director

Mark McKenna is Eleanor's eldest son, and he has been Managing Director since 2014. Mark grew up on the Waikato farm and joined the family business in his twenties. As MD he leads the day-to-day across both islands, drives our cage-free investment programme, and represents Aurora at industry forums. Mark is a member of the New Zealand Egg Producers Federation and a frequent speaker on the future of pastoral and free-range egg production.

The next generation

Aurora is a generational business. We invest with the next generation in mind - in our farms, in our hens, and in the people who care for them. We're proud to have several members of the McKenna family's third and fourth generations now working in the business, learning the trade from the team and from each other.

Our Eggs

Every Aurora egg is laid by a hen on one of our two New Zealand farms. We farm across colony, barn, and free-range systems to give Kiwi consumers the choice that suits their kitchen and their budget - and we hold every system, regardless of price tier, to the same Aurora standard for hen welfare, feed quality, and freshness.

Free-range - our flagship

Aurora free-range eggs are laid by hens with daily access to open paddocks across our Waikato and Canterbury farms. Our free-range range has been on New Zealand supermarket shelves for more than two decades, and over that time it has consistently commanded a meaningful retail premium over commodity-tier eggs - a premium that reflects what shoppers are prepared to pay for hens raised the way nature intended. Demand for premium free-range eggs continues to grow at pace, and our free-range flock is growing with it.

Barn - quality, indoors

Our barn-laid range is for shoppers who want a cage-free egg at an everyday price. Our modern barn housing gives hens the room to roam, perch and dust-bathe indoors - exactly what cage-free is meant to be. We are investing significantly in new barn housing as part of our 2027 transition, and barn will be a growing share of the Aurora flock through 2026 and 2027.

Colony - the everyday Kiwi egg

Aurora colony-laid eggs remain a popular choice for many Kiwi families and food businesses looking for an affordable, high-quality egg. Our colony housing fully complies with New Zealand's MPI Code of Welfare for Layer Hens. As the country transitions to fully cage-free shell eggs by 2027, we are investing in barn and free-range capacity to convert our colony flock into the cage-free systems that retailers and consumers are asking for.

Pasture-raised specialty (coming soon)

We are excited to be developing a small Aurora pasture-raised line for premium retailers and specialty grocers - to be launched in 2027. Watch this space.

From farm to fridge

Every Aurora egg is graded, candled and packed at our on-farm facilities and shipped fresh to supermarket shelves and foodservice kitchens across both islands. We supply long-standing partners across both major New Zealand supermarket groups under supply relationships that go back decades, plus a wide network of cafés, restaurants, bakeries and food manufacturers.

Our cage-free roadmap to 2027

By the end of 2027, every Aurora shell egg sold through New Zealand's major supermarket groups will come from a cage-free hen. This is the commitment we have made to our retail partners and our consumers - and we are well on our way to delivering it.

What cage-free means at Aurora

Cage-free, in the New Zealand context, means barn-laid or free-range - hens housed in modern barn systems with the freedom to walk, perch, dust-bathe, lay in a nest box and express natural behaviours, or out on pasture in our free-range systems. The major New Zealand supermarket groups have committed to selling only cage-free shell eggs by the end of 2027, and Aurora is committed alongside them.

Where we are today

Aurora has been investing in cage-free housing for years. Our free-range flock has grown steadily since the early 2000s. Our barn capacity is expanding. And our planning for the remaining colony-to-barn conversions across both farms is well advanced, with conversion schedules drawn up and capital committed by the family board.

How we are investing

Our cage-free transition is funded from within the family business, with no external borrowings required. We are investing in:

  • New barn housing on both our Waikato and Canterbury farms, designed to modern cage-free welfare standards
  • Expanded free-range capacity, including additional pasture rotation paddocks at our Canterbury farm.
  • Upgraded grading, packing and traceability infrastructure to handle a higher mix of cage-free product.
  • Training and support for our farm teams to operate the new systems.

On track for 2027

We are working hand-in-hand with our retail partners to make the 2027 transition smooth - for hens, for farmers, and for consumers. Our cage-free programme is on track to meet retailer expectations on time. We will continue to keep our customers and consumers informed of our progress on this News page.

Sustainability & Animal Welfare

Animal welfare has been at the heart of Aurora since 1954. We farm to the New Zealand Ministry for Primary Industries' Code of Welfare for Layer Hens, and we hold ourselves to standards that go beyond the regulatory baseline wherever we sensibly can.

Hen welfare

Our hens are housed in systems that allow them to express natural behaviours. Our barn and free-range hens have continuous access to perches, nest boxes, and litter for dust-bathing. Our free-range hens have daily pop-hole access to outdoor pasture. We work with independent veterinarians on flock health programmes, and our farm teams undergo continuous training in low-stress hen handling.

Feed and traceability

Aurora hens are fed New Zealand-sourced grain blends formulated to meet their nutritional needs at every stage of lay. Every Aurora egg can be traced from carton back to flock, house and lay date through our on-farm grading system.

Looking ahead

Sustainability at Aurora means farming in a way that the next generation of McKennas - and the next generation of Kiwi consumers - would be proud of. Our cage-free 2027 programme is the most visible part of that, but it is one chapter in a longer story.
